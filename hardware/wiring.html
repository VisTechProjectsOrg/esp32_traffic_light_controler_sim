Bench reference

Signal Head Wiring

Vehicle head, pedestrian combo and countdown - seven switched hots across two SSR boards.

Everything here is 120 VAC. The order of work matters: buses first, ring out the colours before trusting them, confirm relay polarity before the mains ever goes live.

What you're working with

Module pigtails are 18 AWG, so every stranded end takes a red fork. Use a ratcheting crimper - a pliers-crimped fork on mains is a genuine failure point.

The AC path

Only the hot and the neutral are bussed. Each relay has two screws: one takes a jumper from the hot strip, the other takes that lamp's wire directly. Never join two lamp wires.

Screw by screw

Follow one lamp: TB1 feeds the relay, the relay feeds the lamp, and its white comes back to TB2.

The green conductor in each ped cord is a switched 120 V hot, not a ground. Mark it with blue tape at both ends.

Both ped modules are 3-wire, which is the ITE standard: orange for the hand, blue for the walking person, white common. The countdown gets its own pair rather than being spliced onto the combo head's orange, because proximity mode flashes that hand symbol for an unrelated warning and a parallel countdown would read it as a clearance interval.

The 5 V side

Relay board DC+ goes to the 5V pin, not 3V3. The two boards draw 320 mA together. The ESP32 ground and both relay board grounds have to be the same rail, or the inputs have nothing to pull down against.

Before mains goes live

  • Ring out every pigtail with the heads unpowered. The ITE colours match these modules, but installer conventions vary and this is the one step firmware can't undo.
  • Confirm LOW = ON on a bare relay channel with a meter. The boards are low-level trigger and the product listing contradicts itself on this.
  • Continuity-check each bus - every screw on TB1 should read as one node, the same for TB2, and no lamp wire should read to another.
  • Fuse the incoming hot and strain-relieve every conductor entering the housing.
  • Fit the clear covers on the barrier strips. They're open-frame at 120 V without them.

Then bring it up with tools/signal_test_gui.py: tick test mode so the cycle can't stomp a manual assertion, and assert one channel at a time to confirm which lamp each one lights. That's the step that catches a swapped orange and blue before the pedestrian phase ever runs.

Worst-case draw is about 0.8 A with every lamp lit and the 5 V block loaded, so a 2 A time-delay fuse holds through SMPS inrush while still protecting 18 AWG comfortably.
